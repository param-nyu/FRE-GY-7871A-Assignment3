### Two bugs Claude found in its own output

Both were caught by the numbers being implausible rather than by tests, and both
materially changed the results:

* The overidentified GMM variance for the `omega_3` estimator divided by the
  sample size twice, producing t-statistics of 6.7 to 18.2 alongside bootstrap
  t-statistics of 0.2 to 2.0. Corrected, the two agree.
* The 2003-versus-2026 direction comparison negated the published Rigobon-Sack
  coefficients, which already describe the response to a war-risk *increase*.
  This had inverted every 2003 direction, and the corrected table tells almost
  the opposite story.

## Decisions I made

Four methodological questions were put to me before the analysis was written, and
a fifth arose from the data:

1. **How to set the sign of the shock.** I chose to determine it empirically from
   escalation days via the three-regime split, rather than assuming the brief's
   2026 prior or copying the paper's 2003 assumption. This makes "yields rise in
   2026" a finding rather than a premise.
2. **Sample window.** Full February 28 to present as the headline, with a
   core-crisis window as the robustness check matching the paper's design.
3. **Authorship of the section 8 essay.** Generated by Claude, disclosed here.
4. **This disclosure's level of detail.** Full and component-level.
5. **The benchmark variable.** When the rank condition failed on the two-year
   Treasury (variance ratio 0.62 against 6.19 in 2003), I chose to renormalise on
   WTI crude and report the failed two-year specification as the diagnostic that
   motivates the switch, rather than either reporting uninterpretable
   coefficients or quietly dropping the replication.

## Scope decisions and limitations

**1. The liquidity premium row is not replicable.** Rigobon and Sack report the
on-the-run/off-the-run ten-year premium, built from proprietary CUSIP-level
quotes. No free equivalent exists, so the row is absent rather than proxied.

**2. Daily rather than intraday data.** Section 8 argues that high-frequency
identification is the best available alternative and that the binding constraint
is the cost of intraday quotes for Treasuries, credit and FX, not the method.

**3. The headline corpus is a capped sample.** GDELT returns at most 250 articles
per query and throttles aggressively. The lexicon and TF-IDF classifier therefore
run on 961 headlines, while the daily signals come from timeline queries over the
full corpus. Some high-volume days carry no sampled headline, which is visible in
Table 1.

**4. The directional classifier is weak.** The two directional measures correlate
only +0.25, and Table 1 shows a clear misclassification (March 31). The report
states this and confines the three-regime split to signing the shock; the
headline results rest on the binary split alone.

**5. Statistical power is poor.** With 42 high-news days, one of nine responses
is significant. The report leads with that rather than burying it.
